On November 25, 2026, Ulys announced an additional $4 million in funding led by Streamlined Ventures, bringing its total funding to $17 million. The AI investing app plans to spend the money mainly on marketing and training its AI models, according to its [funding announcement](https://www.theblock.co/press-releases/ai-investing-app-ulys-reaches-17-million-in-total-funding-446731). Investors include Tom Lee of Fundstrat, BitMine and XBTO Ventures.

The company's [post announcing the raise](https://x.com/Ulys_AI/status/2125707775493718238) leads with the cumulative funding figure. The release separates the new round from that total and says the app launched publicly on the App Store on November 10. Ulys already offers a non-custodial wallet where users can trade tokenized assets and choose to put eligible funds into third-party onchain lending.

## From an investment idea to an approved trade

A user starts by describing an investment view in ordinary language. Ulys researches that view, builds a basket of assets and shows the reasoning and sources behind each selection. The release describes baskets spanning stocks, ETFs, gold and crypto. Before authorizing a purchase, users can review the proposed allocations and ask for changes to the basket in chat.

The company's [product page](https://ulys.ai/) describes an assistant that can suggest buying, selling, holding, adding or trimming a position. Ulys also warns that the assistant's answers can be wrong or out of date. Users can open the sources attached to a recommendation and check them before deciding whether to act on the AI's proposal.

For an ordinary trade, the app shows the asset, amount and fee before execution. The user can change the proposal, approve it or cancel it. Conditional orders work differently. The user gives permission when creating the order, so execution does not require a fresh confirmation when the condition is met.

A smart order can wait for an asset price or a change in an event probability on a prediction market. Ulys says it executes only on the exact terms the user authorized in advance. Users can cancel an order before it executes. Once approved, the instruction allows the app to trade when the specified condition is met, without sending the user back to the chat to approve it.

Placing a smart order reserves no funds in the wallet. The balance can remain invested while the instruction waits for its condition to be met. If the required balance is unavailable when that happens, the order may fail or execute only partly. The product page discloses this funding constraint alongside its description of automated trading: permission to execute an order does not set aside the money needed to complete it.

## What the wallet holds and authorizes

The assets marketed as stocks, ETFs and commodities include third-party tokens that track those assets' prices. Ulys states: "A token is not the stock or fund itself and does not give you shareholder rights." Availability depends on location. Users can place those tokens alongside crypto in a basket, with exposure to the underlying issuer and the rights attached to each instrument.

Ulys describes its account as non-custodial. Funds stay in the user's wallet, and the company says it cannot move them without permission. The wallet uses Privy, a Stripe company. According to Ulys, Privy splits the key into encrypted pieces held in secure hardware. Users can log in with Apple, Google, email or a phone number, without having to write down a seed phrase.

The product page offers an optional private-key export. Users who keep the app's login-based arrangement can recover from losing a phone by logging back in, Ulys says. The company separately describes the permissions for trading and lending. Creating a smart order or switching on Earn gives limited permission for that action, and Ulys says users can remove that permission in the app.

Earn is off by default. When a user enables it, the feature deposits eligible $USDC into third-party onchain lending protocols within the permission that user approved. Those protocols pay the rewards. Ulys displays the current rate in the app and lets users turn Earn off. The displayed rate can change or fall while the feature is in use.

Ulys's lending disclosure says the deposited stablecoin is not a bank deposit and carries no deposit insurance. A protocol failure can cause a loss of funds, including principal. The funding announcement concerns money raised for the app's operator. Its product documentation attributes Earn returns to the third-party lending protocols and describes the risk of losing funds deposited into them.

## Funding and withdrawing the account

Users can fund the wallet by receiving assets from another wallet or buying $USDC through a third-party payment partner with a card or Apple Pay. The app supports outgoing transfers to wallet addresses and lets users send to a contact by phone number. Users can also cash out to a bank through a payment partner where that service is supported. The partner can require identity verification before processing a deposit or withdrawal.

The [withdrawal guide](https://help.ulys.ai/hc/en-us/articles/37151734308251-How-Do-I-Withdraw-Assets) sets out the steps for withdrawing to a bank. A user starts from the Withdraw control or asks in chat, then supplies the amount of $USDC to withdraw. The app redirects the user to the payment partner to complete its prompts. The user then returns to Ulys, where the transaction is created and signed to send. Completing the partner's screens still leaves that wallet transaction to finish.

Ulys publishes a list of countries whose phone numbers can be used to sign up. It separately says existing accounts from other countries can continue to be used if they have already deposited or traded. The company excludes sanctioned countries. Asset access, Earn and card deposits also vary by where the customer lives, so the app displays the features available to that customer.

Ulys has earmarked the new capital mainly for distribution and AI model training. Its funding release gives no timetable for widening country access or changing the lending protocols available through Earn.
