On November 2, 2026, Securitize [expanded its stock offering to 25 equities](https://investors.securitize.io/news/news-details/2026/Securitize-Expands-Onchain-U-S--Stock-Offering-to-25-Equities/default.aspx), adding 13 U.S. companies for eligible investors in the United States, European Union and other permitted jurisdictions. The securities trade on Solana and settle in $USDC, with liquidity infrastructure supported by Jump Trading.

The additions follow the [October 8 launch of Securitize Stocks](/securitize-launches-tokenized-us-stocks-on-solana.html), which introduced 12 equities. The expansion adds Arista Networks, Flex, Exxon Mobil, GameStop, Johnson & Johnson, Lockheed Martin, Lumentum, Merck, Salesforce, Seagate Technology, Texas Instruments, Walt Disney and Uber Technologies.

The new names join the original offering identified in the announcement by the tickers $AAPL, $MSFT, $NVDA, $GOOG, $TSLA, $META, $AMZN, $NFLX, $CRCL, $SPCX, $MSTR and $PLTR. Access remains subject to investor eligibility. Securitize's trading documentation requires account approval and a whitelisted wallet before an investor can trade.

## What the token holder owns

Securitize describes each token as a security entitlement under UCC Article 8, backed by an underlying share. Applicable economic benefits include dividends and voting rights where the underlying share class carries them. The [product page](https://securitize.io/investments/stocks) says the backing share is held in a segregated account and that the shares backing the tokens are never lent out.

Dividends reach investors net of applicable withholding. The legal holding arrangement depends on jurisdiction: U.S. customers hold security entitlements through Securitize Markets, while EU customers hold their interests through Securitize Europe Brokerage and Markets under its client terms and applicable EU and Spanish law.

Token holders become registered shareholders of the underlying issuer only if they convert. Securitize calls the instrument a convertible entitlement token. Conversion into shares on a company's register depends on that company adopting issuer-sponsored tokenization, with Securitize's transfer-agent partnerships providing the route.

The companies behind the newly available stocks have not sponsored, endorsed or issued the tokens. The offering creates no new shares. Secondary trading runs through Securitize Markets, an SEC-registered broker-dealer and FINRA/SIPC member. Securitize says this activity operates under existing securities laws without needing the SEC's Innovation Exemption.

## Approved wallets and protected market orders

The [trading FAQ](https://stocks.securitize.io/faq) specifies third-party self-custody wallets. Investors must create a Securitize account, complete identity verification and connect a wallet after approval. Securitize then whitelists the address. Securities can move only between whitelisted wallets on the platform. Transfers remain subject to approval even when investors hold the assets in their own Solana wallets.

Orders enter as market orders, with execution at the best available market price subject to price protection. The confirmation screen shows a reference based on the National Best Bid and Offer, not a guaranteed execution price. The fixed protection band is 0.10%, and users cannot change it.

The platform automatically rejects an order if the execution price moves beyond the protected level: higher for a purchase or lower for a sale. An investor can enter a rejected order again. Submission does not guarantee execution at the displayed reference: the order can still be rejected after the investor submits it.

The platform charges .10% of the trade amount in $USDC, according to the FAQ. That fee covers the gas charges associated with execution. Share prices are also expressed as a $USDC exchange rate per share; the documentation notes that the stablecoin's exchange rate against the dollar can vary.

Quotes refresh every 30 seconds on the order screen and every 8 seconds in the confirmation window. When the stock price changes during order generation, the share quantity changes while the total $USDC spend remains fixed. An investor committing a fixed amount can receive a different number of shares as the price changes during confirmation.

Creating an order also requires wallet authentication. The FAQ instructs users to sign in through their wallet provider, specify the $USDC amount and review the reference price, estimated share quantity and total spend. After accepting the conditions, the investor receives a single wallet-provider request to approve the transaction, which must be confirmed before proceeding.

After submission, the platform says it notifies the user whether the transaction succeeded or the order was rejected. Successful execution prices and times are available through Securitize, on the blockchain and in an email confirmation sent at execution. Investors can use those records to compare the completed trade with the reference they saw before approving it.

## Trading hours need clarification

Securitize's public documents give different descriptions of current availability. The investment product page advertises 24/5 trading, with 24/7 coming soon. The trading FAQ states: “Trading is available on our site from 4:00 AM to 5:30 PM ET.” The November 2 release says trading is initially available beyond traditional market hours and describes continuous availability as a plan.

The product page also says trading hours, supported stocks and conversion features are subject to change. Investors cannot establish a consistent current schedule from these pages alone. The [disclosures on the Exxon Mobil asset page](https://stocks.securitize.io/asset/XOM) warn that extended-hours sessions can bring lower liquidity, wider bid/ask spreads, greater volatility and less market visibility.

The settlement asset carries separate limitations. Securitize's product disclosure says $USDC is neither legal tender nor a bank deposit and is not protected by the Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation or the Securities Investor Protection Corporation. The broker's stated memberships should not be read as insurance for the stablecoin used to fund a stock purchase.

Securitize reserves the right to withdraw listings from venues as needed or required, according to the release's disclosures. The announcement also names future trading venues, but its disclosures say the NYSE and OKXICE venues have not launched. Trading there remains subject to regulatory approval and venue review and may not occur; the release gives no firm start date for either venue.
