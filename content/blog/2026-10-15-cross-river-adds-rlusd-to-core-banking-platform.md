On October 15, 2026, Cross River [announced an integration](https://www.crossriver.com/newsroom/cross-river-embeds-ripple-usd-stablecoin-on-the-xrp-ledger-into-its-core-banking-infrastructure-expanding-faster-payment-capabilities-for-businesses) that will put Ripple USD ($RLUSD) on XRPL inside the bank's proprietary Core Operating System. The bank says fintechs will reach the stablecoin and traditional payment rails through one API connection.

The announcement describes $RLUSD as an additional payment rail rather than a separate customer application. Cross River will support stablecoin payments alongside ACH, wires and real-time payments. Customers will not need to deploy their own blockchain infrastructure to use the new route.

Cross River did not publish a production date. Its release says services are currently available only in limited states, without naming those states or specifying whether that limitation applies to every part of the integration. The announcement also omits pricing, transaction limits, onboarding requirements and the first customers.

## How the payment route works

Cross River's technology platform is the integration layer between regulated financial services and blockchain payment networks. The new work connects that layer to $RLUSD on XRPL. Fintechs using the bank's platform will be able to move between U.S. dollars and $RLUSD through the same banking relationship used for traditional payment methods.

The practical route has several possible endpoints. A customer can use $RLUSD for cross-border payments or treasury movement, then access ACH, wires or real-time payments from Cross River's platform. The release also says issuers and acquirers can use the rail as a new settlement option. Cross River and Ripple expect that option to support intraday settlement and reduce latency between payment counterparties.

The ledger provides the continuous part of the setup. Cross River says XRPL transactions settle in 3-5 seconds, cost a fraction of a cent and can move 24/7. The same release says the ledger has settled more than $1.3 trillion in value to date. Those network properties do not mean each connected banking rail runs continuously. ACH and wire availability still depends on the bank's service and the relevant external rail.

Adam Goller, Cross River's head of fintech banking, said the integration builds on stablecoin capabilities already under development. He named movement between U.S. dollars and $RLUSD, cross-border settlement, treasury movement and access to existing payment rails as the functions customers will receive through one platform and API connection.

That arrangement gives an operator one technical entry point, but it does not collapse the underlying assets or settlement systems into one. $RLUSD remains a dollar-backed stablecoin issued on a public blockchain. ACH, wires and real-time payments remain conventional payment routes. Cross River's system handles access between them and presents the available routes through its API.

## What changes for fintechs and payment firms

A fintech that already uses Cross River will no longer need to build and operate a separate XRPL integration merely to access $RLUSD. The bank says customers can use the stablecoin through its API without deploying blockchain infrastructure. That can remove wallet, node and ledger connectivity work from the customer's own product stack, although the release does not describe custody arrangements or responsibility for key management.

For a treasury team, the new rail adds an option outside standard banking windows. $RLUSD on XRPL can move around the clock, while Cross River's platform provides the path back to dollars and bank payment systems. This can help a business position funds for cross-border or treasury transfers when a conventional rail is closed. The announcement does not state how quickly an onchain balance becomes available for an ACH, wire or real-time payment.

Issuers and acquirers get a similar choice on the settlement side. The companies describe $RLUSD as an option for intraday settlement, which could shorten the interval between collecting and disbursing funds. The release does not identify a card network, merchant acquirer or issuer using that flow at launch. It also does not explain reserve, reconciliation or reversal procedures when an onchain transfer connects to a payment that can be returned or disputed.

The integration arrives with $RLUSD at more than $2.5 billion in market capitalization, according to Cross River. The bank says more than $1 billion of that amount is issued on XRPL. This chain split matters because the announced support is specifically for $RLUSD on XRPL. The release does not say that Ethereum-issued $RLUSD can enter the same route or be bridged automatically.

Cross River presents its Core Operating System as the common environment for stablecoin and traditional payment access. For customers, the immediate operational question is which functions are live when access is approved. The language ranges from "customers can now access $RLUSD" to capabilities that are being expanded or developed. Without a launch schedule or named customer deployment, operators still need confirmation of production availability for their state and use case.

## The open implementation details

The announcement establishes the asset, ledger and banking connection, but leaves the commercial and risk terms open. It does not give API documentation, supported transaction sizes, fees, foreign-exchange terms, custody design or service-level commitments. It also does not identify the compliance checks applied when funds move between an XRPL address and a bank account.

Markus Infanger, senior vice president at RippleX, said the objective is to place stablecoins inside banking infrastructure that businesses already use. That framing matches the technical division in the release: Ripple supplies $RLUSD and the ledger, while Cross River supplies the regulated banking platform and connections to conventional rails.

The next verifiable milestone will be a production rollout with named availability. Cross River has not dated that milestone. Fintechs considering the rail will need the bank to specify eligible states, account requirements and which API functions are live before they can treat around-the-clock $RLUSD movement as an available extension of their existing payment stack.
