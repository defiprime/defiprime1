On October 27, 2026, Bitget Wallet [announced dedicated USD and EUR virtual accounts](https://x.com/BitgetWallet/status/2115050943557537966) for receiving bank transfers inside its wallet. Incoming funds convert automatically into $USDC or $USDT. Eligible users can receive salary, client payments and family transfers as stablecoins. Payment-network fees and compliance reviews apply to the incoming money.

The product's [help center](https://web3.bitget.com/en/helpCenter/872) describes a dedicated receiving service. It is not a conventional personal bank account: cash deposits, cash withdrawals and other standard banking operations are unavailable. The product page says regulated financial partners provide the account services, a qualification to the announcement's claim of "No banks."

## Account details depend on currency and region

Approved users receive account details in their own name. USD accounts have an account number and routing number for ACH, Fedwire and SWIFT transfers. EUR accounts use an IBAN and BIC for SEPA. Availability varies by region, according to the help center. Some users may receive only one currency account or access to only certain clearing networks. Users must check the in-app Virtual Account page to see what their country supports.

The [application instructions](https://web3.bitget.com/en/helpCenter/874) require applicants to live in a supported jurisdiction, verify their identity in Bitget Wallet, submit the requested documents and receive approval. Opening an account is free. Applicants start from the Virtual Account page, complete authentication and submit their documents. Bitget says the usual review takes a few minutes. Users then choose the stablecoin and network on which they want to receive funds. The account details appear after that selection.

Compliance checks cover identity, the intended use of the account and the source of funds. Bitget says it keeps the information confidential and uses it solely for compliance purposes. Applications that need additional review follow a different timetable from the ordinary approval flow. The service provider emails the address associated with the verified identity and asks for supplementary documents, usually within 10 working days. Applicants who miss that submission window fail to open an account.

Senders must use the beneficiary and bank details shown for their selected payment route. The [transfer instructions](https://web3.bitget.com/helpCenter/876) list the beneficiary name, bank name, account number and routing number for ACH. Fedwire also requires the bank address. SWIFT uses the account number, SWIFT/BIC and bank address. For SEPA, senders need the beneficiary name, bank name, IBAN and BIC. Incorrect details can delay a payment or cause it to be returned.

## Free opening, fees on each deposit

Each deposit carries a fixed transaction charge plus a percentage of the amount, followed by a network fee for conversion into $USDC or $USDT. Bitget deducts the equivalent value of those fees in the stablecoin. The sending bank or payment provider may charge separately. Bitget says those third-party charges are outside its control, and its fee schedule directs users to the account page for the specific fees and final amount received.

For USD transfers, ACH costs a fixed 0.5 USD plus 0.3%, with a stated arrival window from a few minutes to 3 business days. Fedwire costs a fixed 14 USD plus 0.3% and has a same-day arrival estimate. SWIFT costs a fixed 30 USD plus 0.3%, with an arrival window from a few minutes to 5 business days. These are the service's published transfer estimates. They do not guarantee that every route credits the wallet immediately.

For EUR transfers, SEPA costs a fixed 0.50 EUR plus 0.3%. Bitget gives an arrival window from a few minutes to 1 business day. The minimum deposit is 50 USD for dollar transfers and 1 EUR for euro transfers. The help center warns that transfers below these minimums may fail, including a small payment sent to test the receiving account.

## Larger transfers can require more documents

The service can request Enhanced Due Diligence when a payment exceeds its published limits or when the provider identifies a higher-risk user or transaction. The amount is only one trigger: a payment below a listed threshold can still require additional information under the provider's risk assessment.

The USD schedule lists non-personal transfers above 5,000 USD, a personal transfer limit of up to 10,000 USD and a cumulative monthly limit of up to 20,000 USD. For EUR, it lists up to 15,000 EUR for an individual transaction and up to 30,000 EUR for the cumulative monthly amount. Bitget publishes these figures as circumstances that may require extra due diligence. Payments below the limits remain subject to review.

Users have 10 working days to respond once the provider requests that information. The help center states: "If you do not respond within this period, the funds will be returned in their original form." This deadline applies to a transfer that has triggered a compliance request, separate from the additional-review window during account opening. Payroll and client payments are subject to both the payment network's arrival estimate and the recipient's response to a document request.

## Returned payments lose the original transfer fees

The [troubleshooting guide](https://web3.bitget.com/en/helpCenter/878) also lists unsupported sender types among the reasons for returning a transfer. Refunds go to the original sending bank account, typically within 10 business days. Bitget charges no additional refund fee. Transaction and bank-channel fees incurred on the original transfer are non-refundable, so the returned amount is what was actually received after those deductions.

For an abnormal deposit or refund, users can check the order details and open a customer-support request there. The guide asks for proof of deposit, the payment bank, the sending bank account and account name, plus the deposit amount and time. It lists bank and intermediary-bank delays, holidays and non-working days as possible reasons for longer processing.

For an EDD delay, the guide tells users to look for email from the service provider, naming Noah, and submit the additional documents on time. The [public product page](https://web3.bitget.com/en/virtual-account) says regulated financial partners provide the account services. The reviewed instructions leave the complete list of supported receiving networks to the in-app selection.
