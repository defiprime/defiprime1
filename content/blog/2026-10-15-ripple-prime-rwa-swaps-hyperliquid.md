On October 15, 2026, Ripple [announced](https://ripple.com/ripple-press/ripple-launches-support-for-institutions-to-access-real-world-asset-perpetual-swaps/) that Ripple Prime had launched real-world asset perpetual swaps for institutional clients. The products reference more than 100 markets and can be traded and settled 24/7. Ripple did not identify a launch time, contract identifier, trading volume or notional amount in the announcement.

The reference markets span equities, equity indices, exchange-traded funds, metals, energy and foreign exchange. Virtu is among the institutional clients already trading the products, according to Ripple. The announcement names the client and product scope, but does not disclose Virtu's positions, collateral balance or trade size.

## How the swaps connect to Hyperliquid

Ripple Prime is the principal in the RWA swaps. The swaps reference perpetual futures contracts listed by Trade.xyz on Hyperliquid, which Ripple described as a decentralized derivatives venue. This means a Ripple Prime client faces Ripple Prime through a single counterparty relationship while the product's reference contracts come from a separate venue.

That structure is narrower than a claim that the institutions themselves are trading directly on Hyperliquid. Ripple's wording places the client transaction inside its prime brokerage: the swaps are traded through Ripple Prime as principal. It places the referenced perpetual futures on Hyperliquid through Trade.xyz. The announcement does not say the RWA swaps are issued on the $XRP Ledger, and it provides no $XRP Ledger transaction or contract address.

The product extends access beyond the operating hours of the traditional markets represented by the contracts. Ripple said the reference markets trade continuously, including weekends. A client can therefore manage exposure to the named traditional asset classes outside normal exchange hours without waiting for the relevant cash market to reopen.

The derivatives do not transfer the referenced shares, funds, metals, energy products or currencies to the client. The release describes swaps that take their reference from perpetual futures. It does not describe delivery or tokenization of the underlying assets. For practitioners, that distinction determines what can be independently checked: Trade.xyz's listed contracts provide the market reference, while Ripple Prime remains the stated principal for the institutional swap.

The reference design also separates three roles. Trade.xyz lists the perpetual futures, Hyperliquid is the derivatives venue named by Ripple, and Ripple Prime enters the RWA swap as principal with its client. A user evaluating venue risk and counterparty risk therefore needs to assess the referenced market and the prime-broker relationship separately. The release confirms the arrangement but does not publish the contractual fallback used if a reference market is unavailable.

## Margin, collateral and counterparty setup

Ripple Prime said clients can cross-margin the RWA swap exposures against other asset classes supported by the prime broker. The company also said clients can use Ripple USD, or $RLUSD, as collateral for RWA swap transactions. Together, those features put the new positions into the same counterparty, risk-management and margin framework as a client's other supported exposures.

Cross-margining is the practical capital change in the release. An institution does not have to treat each RWA swap position as a completely separate collateral account if Ripple Prime supports the other offsetting or related asset class. Ripple describes the result as consolidated margin across the client's portfolio. The release does not publish the applicable haircuts, margin formulas, liquidation thresholds or eligible offset rules, so a client still needs those terms from Ripple Prime before comparing the product with direct venue access.

The $RLUSD collateral option has a jurisdictional limit. Ripple states that $RLUSD availability may differ by jurisdiction and directs clients to their Ripple Prime representative. The release does not say whether $RLUSD is the sole stablecoin accepted for these swaps, what collateral conversion rules apply, or whether availability matches every market covered by the CIV BVI offering.

Virtu described the cross-margining feature from the client side. Scotte Moegling, its head of business development for digital assets, said Ripple Prime's "24/7 cross-margining across asset classes helps us expand our presence more efficiently as markets move toward round-the-clock access." Ripple did not identify any other participating institution in the announcement.

Ripple framed continuous access as a way for institutions to respond to market developments, manage positions and deploy capital across traditional and digital asset markets. That ability may matter most outside the hours of the referenced cash market. The announcement does not explain how Ripple Prime handles price formation, risk limits or collateral calls when a cash market is closed but its related perpetual market continues trading.

## Availability and what remains open

The offering is currently available through Ripple Prime's CIV BVI license. Ripple said it intends to offer the product from additional regions in the near future, but it did not name those regions or provide a date. The current legal entity matters because access, client eligibility and the availability of $RLUSD collateral can differ across jurisdictions.

The announcement says institutional clients can respond to market developments, manage positions and deploy capital across traditional and digital asset markets on a continuous schedule. It also says the same setup gives clients centralized risk management through one counterparty. Those are product capabilities claimed by Ripple, not evidence that every referenced market has continuous liquidity at the same depth or spread. The release provides no order-book statistics, open interest, execution costs or weekend liquidity data.

For operators, the immediate diligence questions are concrete. They need the list of more than 100 reference markets, the contract specifications used by Trade.xyz, Ripple Prime's margin and liquidation terms, and the rules governing $RLUSD collateral in their jurisdiction. None of those detailed schedules appears in the release. They also need to know whether portfolio offsets remain available during stressed markets and how the prime broker values a referenced contract when the corresponding traditional venue is closed.

The next milestone is regional expansion, although Ripple supplied no date. Until it names the additional regions and publishes their eligibility and collateral terms, the verified scope remains the CIV BVI offering, with Ripple Prime as principal, Trade.xyz perpetual futures on Hyperliquid as references, and 24/7 trading and settlement for the institutional RWA swaps.
