At 15:59 UTC on October 16, 2026, Token Terminal [launched Fact Sheets](https://x.com/tokenterminal/status/2111124879504453931) for more than 10,000 tokenized assets. The product adds legal, operational, and economic disclosures to the platform's existing onchain records. Each asset can now carry more than 60 standardized fields, with citations that point users back to the underlying documents.

The launch separates two questions that token data often mixes together. Blockchain records can show supply, holders, transfers, and activity across chains and DeFi venues. They do not by themselves establish the legal issuer, the holder's claim, the party holding the underlying asset, or the conditions for redemption. Token Terminal's [product announcement](https://tokenterminal.com/resources/newsletter/introducing-token-terminal-fact-sheets) places those details beside the onchain record.

## What the Fact Sheets contain

The fields cover asset identity and classification, issuer and legal structure, service providers, primary-market terms, economics, and assurance. Token Terminal says it has structured more than 750k individual disclosure fields across all tokenized assets on its platform. The coverage therefore goes beyond a directory of token names or contract addresses. It organizes the terms that determine what a token represents and who stands behind it.

The issuer section answers who issued the asset and where that entity is domiciled. Other fields identify which regulator oversees the issuer and which law governs the terms. The asset section describes the claim represented by the token. It can also record who holds the underlying assets and whether the holder receives dividends or other distributions. Redemption fields state whether redemption is available, who can use it, and on what terms.

Token Terminal says every answer is sourced. The cited materials include official issuer documentation, contracts, legal agreements, government and regulatory filings, and public records. The company also works with issuers to verify details and capture information that the documents may not fully explain. That distinction matters for diligence: an issuer confirmation can clarify a field, while the linked source lets a user inspect the basis for the answer.

Each field is interactive in the Token Terminal interface. Selecting an answer opens the platform's interpretation, exact citations from the source, and a direct link to the original document. Users do not have to accept a normalized value without seeing where it came from.

This design preserves a trail from a market-wide screen back to the disclosure behind one result. A user can start with the common fields, inspect Token Terminal's reading of a term, and then open the issuer document or filing. Token Terminal also says it works with issuers when the public record does not capture enough detail. The published citation remains available beside that additional verification, rather than disappearing behind the standardized answer.

The announcement uses a tokenized NVIDIA asset issued by Robinhood as its example Fact Sheet. It also describes a broader screen for tokenized stocks: an analyst could filter for issuers domiciled in Jersey, an issuer-sponsored claim type, and monthly attestations. The resulting set could then be compared by onchain supply, holder counts, transfers, or growth. Token Terminal gives a 30-day change in holder count as one example of the analysis that the combined dataset supports.

## How access will work

Fact Sheet data is available through the Token Terminal interface and Data Catalog. Token Terminal says access is rolling out to its API and MCP server. Pilot users already have those integrations, while general availability is scheduled for the coming weeks. The announcement does not provide a calendar date for that wider release.

The Data Catalog covers the same set of more than 10,000 assets. Programmatic access changes the use case from checking one asset at a time to screening the market by legal and operating terms. Token Terminal says the structured catalog can expose market-wide patterns that are difficult to find by reviewing assets one at a time. A risk team could filter by domicile, claim type, redemption rules, or attestation frequency before joining those results to supply and transfer data. An allocator could compare assets that look similar onchain but give holders different contractual rights.

The fields also put contract-level controls next to offchain terms. Token Terminal lists token standards, transfer restrictions, issuance history, supply, holders, and flows among the onchain facts available for comparison. Those records can be read beside the issuer, service-provider, economics, and assurance fields. The combined view does not turn a disclosure into a guarantee. It makes the source and the platform's interpretation visible enough to check.

For issuers, the common format creates a concrete list of disclosures that market participants can compare. Token Terminal says its goal is to ask every issuer the same questions and structure the answers in the same way. That can reveal differences that a project page or token contract does not show, such as who may redeem, which law controls the agreement, and whether an attestation follows a recurring schedule.

For DeFi operators, the useful boundary is between transferability and enforceable rights. A token may move through a venue while redemption, distributions, or access to the underlying asset depend on offchain eligibility and legal terms. The Fact Sheets make those conditions queryable alongside venue activity. Operators still need to read the cited documents and decide whether the disclosed structure fits their collateral, listing, or risk policy.

The immediate open question is coverage quality across the full catalog. Token Terminal reports more than 750k structured fields, but its announcement does not state how many fields are populated for each asset or how often disclosures will be refreshed. The next stated milestone is general API and MCP availability in the coming weeks.
