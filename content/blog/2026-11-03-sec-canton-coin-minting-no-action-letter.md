On November 3, 2026, the SEC's Division of Corporation Finance issued a [no-action response for Canton Coin](https://www.sec.gov/rules-regulations/no-action-interpretive-exemptive-letters/division-corporation-finance-no-action/canton-coin-110326), saying staff would not recommend registration enforcement against its programmatic minting under the circumstances presented. The response covers Super Validators, Validators and application providers minting the token in reliance on counsel's opinion. Staff also described Canton Coin as a “digital commodity.”

The response names Section 5 of the Securities Act and Section 12(g) of the Exchange Act. It addresses issuance through the existing reward mechanism, through which operators earn Canton Coin for network activity. The relief rests on facts submitted by Digital Asset's counsel, including how participants earn rewards and who can change the protocol.

Staff answered an [October 30 request submitted on behalf of Digital Asset](https://www.sec.gov/files/corpfin/no-action/canton-incoming-103026.pdf), signed by Jeffrey T. Dinwoodie. The request describes the network, its fee-burning system and its governance arrangements. These are the applicant's representations to the SEC, not an independent audit of the network or readings of its current state.

## What the staff accepted

Staff singled out the representation that Canton Coin derives its value from the network's programmatic operation and supply and demand, rather than expected profits from someone else's essential managerial efforts. It also relied on the representation that the network has no “central party” under the interpretation referenced in the response.

The qualification is explicit: “Any different facts or conditions might require the Division to reach different conclusions.” The response states a staff enforcement position and expressly disclaims a legal conclusion on the issues presented. It covers the described minting activity, not every transaction involving the token.

According to counsel, Canton Coin had no initial coin offering, pre-mine, pre-launch sale or allocation. Participants have minted the token through the protocol based on network activity since the network's June 2024 launch. They have used it to pay Global Synchronizer transaction fees. The request says the network was functional when minting began.

Holding Canton Coin does not, under the applicant's representations, accrue yield or confer rights to income, profits or assets of Digital Asset, the Canton Foundation, validators or application providers. The token carries no passive income rights. Counsel's case for the reward mechanism rests on the participant's own work on the network.

## How participants earn and spend the token

The request describes a minting process that runs in discrete rounds. Qualifying activity creates records, which the protocol summarizes and assigns minting weights. Participants can then mint the portion corresponding to their activity. Rewards are unequal within each participant category: the mechanism measures each participant's contribution instead of dividing an allocation equally among everyone in that category.

Validators process and validate transactions to which they are parties, then route those transactions for sequencing and confirmation, according to the filing. Their minting rights are proportional to the fees they burn. The protocol uses those burned fees as a proxy for activity, so earning the rewards requires using the network.

Application providers earn rewards through activity markers generated by their contract code and fees burned through their applications. An application's share of network fees in a round can determine its corresponding share of the application reward allocation in the next round. Under the description supplied to staff, an application that generates no activity earns nothing.

Featured applications seeking enhanced rewards must satisfy lock-up requirements and other eligibility criteria. The request says those conditions are intended to sustain participation and discourage reward extraction without a corresponding contribution. It also says five percent of mintable Canton Coin has been allocated to a Development Fund since February 2026. That allocation comes proportionately from the Super Validator, Validator and application provider allocations.

Network fees burn Canton Coin. None of those burned tokens goes to Digital Asset, the Canton Foundation or another recipient, according to the filing. Traffic fees depend on the Global Synchronizer resources consumed, including bandwidth, storage and related usage. Protocol formulas calculate those fees; participants do not negotiate them with one another.

Fees are denominated in dollars, though payment destroys Canton Coin. The number of tokens needed to cover a fee changes with the token's price. An application funding transactions pays those dollar-denominated resource charges by burning tokens, while its activity determines its eligibility to mint rewards under the mechanism described in the request.

## Who can change the rules

As of September 30, the request lists 13 Super Validator Node Operators. Only those operators vote on governance matters. Each has one vote, regardless of the Super Validators it hosts or the Canton Coin it holds or has earned. Digital Asset operates two Super Validator Nodes, according to the filing. These governance representations form part of the basis for staff's reliance on the absence of a central party.

Proposals require approval by at least two-thirds of the Super Validator Node Operators after review and revisions. Global Synchronizer participants then implement and deploy the approved changes. Under the protocol governance described in the request, votes belong to the node operators. Accumulating Canton Coin does not give a holder voting power.

Counsel says the core code is open source and parties can deploy applications and infrastructure without third-party approval. Application-layer smart contracts configure permissioning and privacy, including who can connect and see data. An application can be configured for public participation and visibility. Defiprime's earlier [technical comparison of Canton and EVM-compatible networks](/canton-vs-evm.html) examines that architectural difference.

Digital Asset also represents that it has not undertaken to support or stabilize Canton Coin's price and makes no promise to do so. Staff relied on that representation alongside the descriptions of network operations and governance. Changed facts or conditions could lead to a different staff conclusion. The response specifies no expiration date for its position.
