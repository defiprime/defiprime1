MEXC will discontinue its Global Card service on November 18, 2026, at 15:30 UTC, stopping transactions on existing cards and suspending new applications. Its [shutdown notice](https://www.mexc.com/announcements/article/mexc-global-card-service-discontinuation-notice-17827791539800) asks cardholders to withdraw remaining balances and replace recurring payments before that deadline.

The exchange attributes the closure to service adjustments by its third-party card issuer. It gives no further explanation of those adjustments. The notice sets out where unused card balances, merchant refunds and fee rebates will go, with each returning through MEXC accounts.

The [Global Card product guide](https://www.mexc.com/learn/article/how-to-use-the-mexc-card-pay-with-crypto-anywhere-visa-is-accepted/1) describes a Visa virtual card funded with $USDT from a user's Fiat account. Users can bring $USDT from an external wallet, but funding the card requires moving it through the exchange: from Spot to Fiat, then onto the card. The shutdown concerns this Global Card product. MEXC's guide lists its APAC card and its ether.fi credit card separately.

## Where balances and refunds go

Before the cutoff, cardholders can select Withdraw on the Global Card page to move their remaining balance into their Fiat account. MEXC also tells users to cancel subscriptions linked to the card or change the payment method. The notice makes no provision for automatically transferring recurring billing arrangements to a replacement card.

Any money left on a card at closure will go to the user's Spot account. MEXC will take a balance snapshot on November 21, 2026, at 15:30 UTC, then credit the corresponding amount within three business days. The exchange says the delay allows pending card transactions to settle. Cardholders do not need to request this transfer or take any other action to receive it.

Merchant refunds go to the Fiat account once MEXC receives them. They are handled separately from the return of unused balances, so a cardholder waiting for both will need to check different accounts. The announcement gives no payout date for merchant refunds and does not include them in the snapshot's payout schedule.

MEXC will credit pending cashback automatically as scheduled. Under the existing product guide, it calculates cashback by calendar month and credits it by the 15th of the following month. Only settled transactions qualify. The shutdown notice leaves that schedule in place, with no announcement of an accelerated cashback payout.

Elite VVIP users with zero-fee benefits have a separate deadline for fee rebates. MEXC says it will return eligible fees already paid to their Spot accounts before December 15, 2026. That deadline applies to the eligible fees, separate from the unused card balances returned after the snapshot and refunds initiated by merchants.

## Pending purchases can still change the balance

MEXC's [transaction FAQ](https://www.mexc.com/support/article/mexc-card-transaction-faq-446452478802094080) explains why a purchase shown on the card may still be pending. During authorization, the issuer checks available funds or credit and places a hold. The merchant has not received the money at that point. The purchase completes when the merchant submits the transaction for settlement and its status changes to settled.

Ordinary settlement takes one to three business days, according to the FAQ, depending on the merchant, acquiring institution and card network. Exceptional cases can take up to 30 days. These are the general transaction windows. MEXC has made no shutdown-specific promise that every outstanding purchase will settle before it takes the balance snapshot.

When an authorization fails, expires or is reversed without settlement, the held amount normally returns automatically. The FAQ puts the usual return window at one to three business days, with no manual action required from the cardholder. This releases money held for an unsettled transaction; a refund concerns a purchase that has already settled.

A foreign-currency purchase can settle for a different amount from the one first displayed. The authorization uses an estimated exchange rate, while the final charge uses the actual settlement rate. MEXC's documentation describes the initial display as a reference amount. Cardholders checking their transaction history against the final balance should use the settled transaction.

Settlement also determines whether a purchase qualifies for cashback. Purchases still in authorization do not qualify yet, and risk-control flags can prevent a credit. If a purchase that earned cashback is later refunded or reversed, MEXC says it deducts the corresponding cashback. The reward a cardholder expects can change while the underlying purchase is being resolved.

## Save records before access expires

Transaction history, card orders and cashback records will remain available on the Global Card page for three months after service ends. MEXC asks users to save any records they may need before access expires. Cardholders disputing a transaction also need those records as part of their supporting evidence.

The transaction FAQ tells customers to find the payment in Transaction History, then contact customer service with a reason for the dispute and supporting documents. These can include correspondence with the merchant, order records and proof of refund. MEXC recommends approaching the merchant first. If that fails, users can file a formal dispute through the exchange.

The deadline depends on the claim. Duplicate-charge disputes have a 90-day limit, while suspected fraud has a 120-day limit. For goods not received or not as described, the 120-day window runs from expected or actual delivery. MEXC warns that it charges a review fee when a dispute is rejected because the information is incomplete or the evidence is insufficient.

The general refund timetable is one to seven days. For e-commerce, airline, hotel and travel transactions, it can extend to as much as 30 days. The closure notice says MEXC will credit refunds when it receives them. It gives no guaranteed arrival date to replace those processing estimates.

MEXC's published fee documents disagree: the transaction FAQ says no refund fee applies to the Global Card, while the separate [fee schedule](https://www.mexc.com/support/article/understanding-the-mexc-card-446453561133852672) lists a 1% Global Card refund fee. The shutdown notice leaves that discrepancy unresolved and does not specify how transactions settling after the balance snapshot will affect the final account credit.
