OKX has launched a [unified EUR and $EURC spot order book](https://www.okx.com/en-eu/learn/okx-unified-eur-eurc-order-book) for eligible European users, with euro-to-$EURC conversion at 1:1. Circle confirmed the service on October 21, 2026, describing a route to trade and manage activity in euro terms.

The change pools liquidity that would otherwise sit in separate fiat and stablecoin markets. Traders choose their settlement asset while using the same supported order book. OKX says customers with euro-funded accounts can trade without routing through dollar-denominated pairs and track profit and loss in their home currency.

Circle's [confirmation](https://x.com/circle/status/2112914304424989060) followed an OKX product FAQ published on October 11. The FAQ sets out app and web instructions, eligibility conditions and fee disclosures for the spot-trading product. Customers can use it where their jurisdiction and the markets available in their account allow access.

## A shared book with a choice of trading asset

OKX says it adapted the routing engine it previously used to consolidate dollar markets. It compares the new arrangement with separate fiat and stablecoin pairings, where orders sit in different books. Supported EUR and $EURC markets now share a single pool, with settlement in either asset. OKX presents tighter spreads and lower slippage as benefits. The announcement gives no measured before-and-after execution results.

The [product FAQ](https://www.okx.com/en-sg/help/eea-eurc-orderbook-circle) places the feature in the exchange's spot interface. In the app, users switch to Exchange mode, open Spot and select a euro pair. On the website, they open Trade, then Spot, then a euro pair. The “Trade with” selector at the top of the trading screen lets them choose euros or $EURC as the trading asset.

Eligible users in supported European Economic Area jurisdictions can access the feature. OKX directs customers to search for EUR or $EURC in the app or on the website to see which pairs are available. Availability varies by region and can change over time. The public FAQ gives no fixed list of markets that every European account can trade.

Conversion at par comes with a separate fee disclosure. Standard trading fees may apply according to the customer's tier and the transaction being completed. OKX says it displays applicable charges before the customer confirms a conversion or trade. The quoted 1:1 conversion rate needs to be read alongside those displayed charges; it does not mean every operation is free.

The FAQ also warns that prices and liquidity can change, and that stablecoins may fail to maintain their intended value. Euro routing removes the need to pass through dollar markets, while the risks described in the FAQ still apply to the assets customers hold and trade.

## What sits behind the stablecoin balance

Circle identifies the issuer of $EURC as Circle Internet Financial Europe SAS, an electronic money institution authorized by France's ACPR. Its [product page](https://www.circle.com/eurc) classifies the token as an e-money token under MiCA. Circle says $EURC is fully euro-backed and publishes monthly reserve attestations for holders to inspect.

Circle Mint also converts euros to and from $EURC. Its ordinary service is aimed at qualified businesses and wholesale distributors, and Circle describes the conversion as near-instant. The OKX launch makes $EURC selectable within supported exchange markets, where customers use it under the exchange's own eligibility and fee rules.

Selling through an exchange and redeeming directly with the issuer have different procedures. Circle's [MiCA redemption policy](https://www.circle.com/legal/mica-redemption-policy) names OKX among exchanges where holders can buy or sell its coins. Holders denied redemption at an exchange, or those who specifically want to redeem with Circle France, must follow the issuer's process. That process also applies when a holder wants to redeem directly with Circle a $EURC balance acquired through the new order book.

## Direct redemption has its own checks

Retail holders apply through a form on Circle France's website. Circle assesses their eligibility and verifies their identity. Applicants must provide accurate information for know-your-customer and transaction checks, along with an IBAN for an EEA bank account. To receive euros from the issuer, a holder needs both the tokens and a qualifying bank destination.

The retail applicant must sign an electronic form confirming that the bank details are correct and that they are the account beneficiary. For $EURC redemption, the account must accept SEPA payments. Under the policy, the customer remains responsible for any currency-conversion or incoming-deposit charges their bank imposes.

Circle then provides a unique deposit address for the coins being redeemed. Once it receives them, it checks the assets and their previous transactions before paying the holder's bank account. The policy says this process should take no more than 5 business days unless discrepancies arise. This timetable covers the issuer's retail redemption process, separate from OKX's conversion feature and Circle Mint's description of its business service.

Non-retail holders must open a Circle Mint account for redemption-only service and agree to Circle France's account terms. Circle's policy also allows its ordinary redemption arrangements to be amended or suspended when a recovery or redemption plan is implemented.

The policy denies redemption to holders classified as restricted persons. If verification reveals inconsistencies or anti-money-laundering risks, Circle conducts further assessment under applicable law. The ordinary processing timetable depends on the holder and transaction passing those checks, as well as Circle receiving the coins.

Circle also excludes copies and wrappers from its support obligations. It accepts responsibility for its own coins, but not independently created assets that claim to incorporate them or be collateralized by them. Direct redemption requires the issuer-supported asset; holders cannot assume the same terms cover a third-party representation.

OKX leaves the complete supported-pair list to the app and website. Its launch documents publish neither a fixed market count nor a quantified execution improvement.
