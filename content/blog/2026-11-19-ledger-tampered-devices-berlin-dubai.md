On November 18, 2026, Ledger expanded its hardware implant investigation beyond Southeast Asia, identifying Berlin and Dubai in an updated security advisory. The company says the physical modifications target recovery phrases during wallet setup: ["It is the same incident. Every device we have confirmed shows the same kind of physical modification, built to capture the recovery phrase during setup."](https://support.ledger.com/article/Update-on-the-Hardware-Implant-Incident-Investigation?redirect=false)

Ledger also states: "There is no flaw in Ledger's secure chip or operating system, and no confirmed case involving a device bought on ledger.com." These are the manufacturer's findings from an investigation that is still running. The advisory covers altered hardware that reached buyers through distribution. Its instructions now reach customers outside the region covered by the company's initial response.

## Different warnings for Berlin, Dubai and Southeast Asia

The German warning names a city and a purchase window. Ledger says it has made progress investigating reports of a few tampered units localized to Berlin. It tells buyers: "As a precaution, if you purchased a Ledger signer in Berlin from January 2026, please reach out to Ledger Support." That instruction covers purchases in the city; the quoted guidance names no particular store.

For the United Arab Emirates, Ledger says: "We are investigating reports of 2 tampered units in Dubai." The company is working with distribution partners to establish how those devices entered circulation and says it will share confirmed findings. It asks Dubai buyers to contact support as a precaution. Unlike the Berlin instruction, the Dubai warning gives no purchase-date cutoff.

In Southeast Asia, Ledger continues to investigate resellers whose sales it had previously paused. The update also identifies an affected model: "We have found one case of a Ledger Nano Gen 5 being impacted." The notice follows the regional incident covered in [Singapore's police advisory about tampered Ledger wallets](/ledger-singapore-police-advisory.html), with Berlin and Dubai now among the locations under investigation.

Ledger had reported clean samples from Germany in its [earlier account of its response](https://www.ledger.com/blog-response-hardware-implant-attack). The company said it sent teams to local markets, including Germany, to buy devices from stores and open them. It also examined warehouse samples. Its reported result at the time was: "Every unit examined was clean." The Berlin warning follows that sampling exercise. The earlier result applied to the units examined then, not the devices now under investigation.

## Replace recovery material, not just the device

The current guidance addresses concerned third-party buyers in Southeast Asia, Berlin and Dubai. Ledger tells them: "Do not open or modify the device yourself." It also gives a setup instruction for a device suspected of tampering: "Do not use it to set up or restore a wallet." Both instructions precede any attempt by the owner to inspect the hardware internally or enter recovery material into it.

For funds already held in an affected wallet, Ledger specifies where the replacement wallet's recovery phrase must come from: "Move your funds to a new wallet whose recovery phrase was created on a device bought directly from ledger.com." The company separately warns: "Do not reuse your current recovery phrase or passphrase, because a tampered device can capture both." The migration instructions call for fresh recovery material, including a fresh passphrase where used, rather than carrying existing credentials onto replacement hardware.

Ledger instructs users to stop using the device and contact local authorities to file a claim, then contact Ledger Support through ledger.com. The published handling instructions put the local-authority report before the support contact. These are the company's directions for concerned owners, alongside its instructions on moving funds and handling the suspected device.

Support can arrange a physical inspection, including X-ray analysis, for devices that raise concerns. Ledger offers the service for a limited time. Users can arrange secure shipment to its factory in Vierzon, France, for inspection and return. The notice directs owners to arrange the process through support rather than take the casing apart themselves.

Ledger also sets a condition for sending in a device: "Ledger will never ask for your 24-word recovery phrase, including Ledger Support, and will never ask you to send a device except through an official Support ticket." The shipment must go through that official ticket process. Support will not request recovery words as part of the inspection.

## How altered devices returned to sale

Ledger's earlier response describes attackers buying genuine devices over many months, opening them and installing custom hidden components. They then returned the devices to reseller distribution in Southeast Asia. The company's Donjon security lab dismantled an implant and identified how it worked. According to Ledger, the attackers had physically altered genuine hardware, adding components designed to collect the recovery phrase during wallet setup.

The company also described infrastructure beyond the devices: "They built their own logistics and technology infrastructure with seeds collector servers, draining and laundering infrastructures." Ledger said it was working with law enforcement and the security community to follow funds and dismantle that infrastructure. The account gives the company's findings; it provides no independently verified loss total for this article.

The earlier response asked authorized resellers and distributors to suspend sales in Southeast Asia and Hong Kong. Ledger said it screened large numbers of samples from its own stock without finding compromised units. It also reminded resellers that returned devices could never be resold in any form. That prohibition applied even to returns that appeared new and unopened.

Ledger's new handling requirements kept products off shelves and displays. Sellers were told to retain stock in the back and hand it over at purchase, and to report customer complaints about unusual products promptly. The current update says the Donjon is examining suspected devices while Ledger reviews distribution records with partners and works with law enforcement and the security community. Resellers have been asked to pause sales wherever inspection remains ongoing.

Ledger says more affected devices may still be found, and it is still reviewing how the reported Dubai units entered circulation. The company says its support advisory will carry further updates and security guidance.
