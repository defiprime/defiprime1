On November 16, Trezor [disclosed a breach at shipping partner Skladon](https://trezor.io/blog/news/data-breach-at-trezor-shipping-partner-skladon) that it believes exposed records for approximately 27,000 orders. The affected orders were placed between August 9 and November 7, 2026. The company warned customers to expect phishing using their purchase details and to be alert to risks to their physical security.

Skladon notified Trezor on November 15 about unauthorized access to systems containing customer shipping data. Trezor dated the breach itself to November 7. The investigation is ongoing. The order count estimates how many records were exported; it is not a count of compromised wallets or confirmed financial losses.

The records included names, phone numbers, delivery addresses, email addresses, order dates, product names and prices. Trezor said: “No wallet backups, PINs or passphrases were involved. Neither Trezor nor Skladon ever holds them.” In its [public statement on X](https://x.com/Trezor/status/2122363569891135655), it also said: “Trezor systems were not compromised, and your device is secure.”

## Who is affected and what to watch for

Trezor emailed affected customers whose contact details it still held. Its notice says customers who received that email and ordered within the stated window had their details exposed. Some customers who ordered between August 9 and August 14 may also be affected, even if they received no email. Trezor could no longer contact them because it had already anonymized their details under its existing retention policy.

The FAQ also addresses customers who received an email despite ordering after November 7. It says they are not affected. Skladon serves multiple countries, so the breach extends beyond one region. Trezor's X statement specifically excludes customers in the United States because Skladon does not ship there. Exposure depends on whether the shipping partner held the order records, not simply on whether a customer owns a Trezor device.

Some customers have already reported scam calls, according to Trezor. Callers or messages may cite the product a customer bought and the price paid to appear legitimate. The company's warning covers phone calls, text messages, emails and physical letters. It tells customers to treat unexpected contact as suspicious even when the message contains accurate purchase information.

Trezor directs customers to contact support through its website. They should not use a link, QR code or phone number supplied in a suspicious message. Legitimate representatives will never cold-call customers or ask for their wallet backup, the company says. Its notice tells users never to share that backup and to enter it only on their Trezor device.

The exposed records pair home addresses with the products purchased. Trezor advises customers to be careful about who knows they own crypto. Anyone receiving a threat or feeling unsafe should contact local police first, the company says. Its disclosure reports scam calls but no device compromise arising from this breach.

This is a separate incident from the [earlier warning to CryptoBilis buyers](/trezor-cryptobilis-implant-warning.html). In the Skladon disclosure, Trezor acknowledges that incidents involving partners have exposed customer contact details and addresses before. It says it is reviewing those relationships and the security requirements it imposes on third parties.

## Less order data and a shorter retention period

Trezor says it informed the data protection authority about the incident. It is also using its blog and X statement to reach the early-August customers whose records it had already anonymized and whom it could no longer email. The company says it will update the disclosure as it learns more.

The company is working to shorten order-data retention from 90 days to 45 days, with further reductions planned. Its pickup documentation says shipping partners must follow the same policy. Trezor describes the reduction as work in progress and has not confirmed in the disclosure that every partner has completed the change. Ordinary shipping still requires it to share essential information with fulfillment partners.

Anonymous Pickup is another part of its response. The incident FAQ says customers in 18 European countries can now order without their name or home address reaching Trezor. The [product's public documentation](https://trezor.io/blog/news/how-to-order-a-trezor-without-sharing-your-personal-information), dated November 13, describes delivery to a local pickup point in unbranded packaging. Trezor had documented the feature before issuing its November 16 breach warning.

Customers using that ordering process can supply an alias and disposable email address. They do not have to provide a real name, phone number, home address or primary email address. The pickup code arrives in the disposable inbox. Support requests, returns and warranty claims use the order ID, so customers retain access to those services when ordering privately, the company says.

The documentation excludes Bulgaria, Cyprus, Denmark, Spain, Greece, Austria, Ireland and Luxembourg from the EU rollout. Germany uses a different service, Discreet Pickup, because customers must pass an identity check when collecting a parcel. They must enter their name exactly as it appears on an official identity document and bring that document with them to collect the order.

German orders still omit the customer's home address and phone number, according to Trezor. The packaging is unbranded, and the label does not describe its contents. The company says it does not store the home address in its fulfillment systems under this process. Customers in Germany cannot use an alias because of the identity requirement.

Customers can choose whether to pay with crypto. Trezor recommends it for those seeking additional payment privacy because a traditional payment processor would otherwise receive information such as a name and billing address. This recommendation concerns the information shared when paying for the order, separately from the delivery details collected through the pickup service.

Trezor says it is working to extend pickup availability to the United States and other regions, but these documents give no US launch date. The investigation into the Skladon breach remains ongoing.
