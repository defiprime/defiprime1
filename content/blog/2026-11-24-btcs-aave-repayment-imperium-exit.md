On November 23, 2026, BTCS [announced that it had repaid $43.8 million of Aave borrowings](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1436229/000149315226053681/ex99-1.htm) after selling approximately 27,300 ETH during the November 20 market sell-off. Its DeFi business, Imperium, also temporarily exited its liquidity pool positions.

The company reported approximately $10.5 million of Aave debt remaining, at a loan-to-value ratio of approximately 32%. Chief executive Charles Allen said the sale was intended to reduce the risk of collateral liquidation and preserve the company's ability to meet its senior secured convertible note obligations. Imperium earns no liquidity pool fees while its capital remains undeployed.

## A smaller loan and a smaller asset base

The [current report submitted to the SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1436229/000149315226053681/form8-k.htm) records the ether sale and repayment under Other Events. BTCS used the approximately $43.8 million in sale proceeds to repay the same amount of Aave borrowings. The transaction took place on November 20. The accompanying press release followed on November 23.

BTCS had already been [selling ether to manage its Aave collateral risk](/btcs-ether-sales-aave-collateral-risk.html). The new disclosure reports a further repayment and an exit from Imperium's liquidity positions. The outstanding debt balance and loan-to-value ratio both come from the company. Neither figure was independently verified against its Aave account.

After the sale and repayment, BTCS reported approximately 24,245 ETH and a combined fair value of approximately $61.0 million for cash and digital assets. It based that valuation on an ether price of $1,722 as of November 22. The company described the balance sheet figures as preliminary and unaudited.

BTCS also reported approximately $17.9 million in aggregate principal of senior secured convertible notes, maturing in May 2027 and July 2027. Those obligations sit alongside the remaining Aave balance. In explaining the sale, Allen named preserving the company's ability to meet the note obligations as one of its objectives.

“Selling at these prices came at a real cost to the Company and its shareholders, and we do not take that lightly,” Allen said in the release. BTCS described a deliberate sale to pay down debt. It did not report that Aave had liquidated its collateral.

BTCS's independent registered public accounting firm has not reviewed the estimates. The company cautioned that they will change with digital asset prices and may differ from the amounts eventually reported in its financial statements. The reported asset value applies to the valuation date given in the disclosure; it is not a current account balance.

## Repayment changes the borrower's liquidation exposure

Aave's [liquidation documentation](https://aave.com/help/borrowing/liquidations) describes the health factor as collateral value multiplied by the weighted average liquidation threshold, divided by total borrow value. Borrowers can improve that metric by supplying more collateral or repaying part of their debt. BTCS funded its repayment by selling assets.

Liquidation becomes possible when a borrower's health factor falls below 1. A liquidator can then repay debt on the borrower's behalf and receive collateral with a liquidation bonus. The borrower surrenders collateral through that process. Allen said the sale was intended to reduce the company's exposure to that risk.

The reported 32% loan-to-value ratio is not a published health factor. Aave's formula also requires the applicable liquidation threshold and collateral values. BTCS's announcement gives a snapshot of its leverage but leaves out the account's health factor and the liquidation price for the remaining position.

Aave liquidations are permissionless: any network participant can initiate one when an account qualifies. The documentation describes competition to react to oracle prices, collateral balances and protocol parameters. BTCS reported a change to its own borrow position. Its announcement describes no change to Aave's liquidation mechanism.

The amount eligible for liquidation depends on position size and health factor. Aave's documentation permits liquidation of up to 50% of debt when the health factor is above 0.95 and both collateral and debt are worth at least $2,000 each. Up to 100% can be liquidated when the health factor is 0.95 or below, or either side of the position is worth less than $2,000. These are the protocol's rules, not a report of a BTCS liquidation.

## Imperium stops earning pool fees

Imperium supplies capital to on-chain liquidity markets, including automated market maker pools on Ethereum and Layer-2 networks. BTCS says the business draws on the protocol expertise of Builder+, its block-building operation, and NodeOps, its staking operation. Imperium withdrew from the liquidity pool positions through which it generates fees.

BTCS said its market indicators did not favor providing liquidity during the sell-off. Management intends to redeploy capital when it judges conditions have improved. The release expressly states that Imperium earns no liquidity pool fees while the capital is undeployed. The withdrawal interrupts that revenue source for as long as the capital stays out of the pools.

Allen described Imperium as the company's best-performing business that year and said BTCS planned to concentrate on it. He said the company had surpassed its full-year gross profit target in nine months on the strength of Imperium. He made that claim as BTCS announced the liquidity withdrawal, without giving a timetable for restarting the activity.

“We are a smaller company today, and we intend to rebuild around that business,” Allen said. BTCS plans to focus its capital and resources on Imperium and says it continues to monitor its remaining digital asset positions. The company may take additional actions as it considers appropriate.

BTCS's risk disclosures identify smart contract vulnerabilities, DeFi protocol failures, counterparty risk, impermanent loss and liquidity risk among Imperium's operating exposures. They also identify concentration risk from the company's Ethereum-first strategy and focus on a single business. Management cautions that the market indicators it uses to decide when to deploy or withdraw capital can prove inaccurate, and that prior results are not indicative of future results.

BTCS gave no date for putting Imperium's capital back into liquidity pools. Its release says there can be no assurance as to when it will redeploy that capital.
