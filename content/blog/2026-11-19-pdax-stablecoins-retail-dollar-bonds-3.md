PDAX has opened a stablecoin funding route for Philippine Retail Dollar Bonds 3, with a public offer scheduled to run until November 24, 2026. Its [announcement](https://x.com/pdaxph/status/2123327309163966717) lets users convert eligible stablecoins into USD* through PDAX FX*, then use that balance to place bond orders starting at USD* 200.

Customers use their stablecoin balances to fund a sovereign securities purchase through PDAX and its securities broker. The bonds sit in the National Registry of Scripless Securities, or NRoSS, and the app displays a corresponding token. PDAX has announced a way to fund bond purchases. The announcement provides no evidence that the Philippines has issued transferable sovereign bonds on Solana.

## From stablecoins to a bond order

PDAX accepts $USDC, $PYUSD, $RLUSD, $USDPT and $USDG, alongside $USD₮ and $USA₮. [Solana's own announcement](https://x.com/solana/status/2123335237866504475) names $PYUSD, $USDG and $USDPT among the accepted stablecoins. These are assets customers can use to fund an order. PDAX documents the securities purchase itself as a separate process, with a conversion to dollars before the broker executes the order.

The [RDB product guide](https://pdax.ph/learn/retail-dollar-bonds-rdbs-pdax/) says PDAX converts the customer's USD* into USD at the prevailing conversion rate. PDAX then sends those dollars to PDAX Securities, the securities broker that executes the buy order. Customers can access the offering through the app without opening a separate US dollar bank account. The broker receives fiat dollars for the purchase, even when the customer started with stablecoins.

The minimum order is USD* 200, with additional amounts in increments of USD* 100. A submitted order still has to go through allocation and issuance. The Bureau of the Treasury finalizes allocations and issues the bonds on the issue date. Once that happens, the app's token status changes from “Order” to “Issued.” Until then, the portfolio displays an order awaiting issuance, rather than an issued holding.

PDAX says it records the holdings in the customer's own NRoSS account. The corresponding RDB token appears under Portfolio > Bonds in the app. The documentation identifies the securities record and tells customers where to find its representation in their portfolio. It supplies no public-chain bond contract in the cited material. Solana's announcement describes the accepted stablecoins; PDAX's registry description explains where the bond holdings are recorded.

## The coupon and the payment path

The announced coupon is 5.65% per year, paid quarterly. PDAX also states: “No withholding tax deductions on interest payouts, with the applicable tax assumed by the BTr.” Under the announced tax treatment, the Bureau of the Treasury assumes the applicable tax on those payments. Customers receive the interest payouts without a deduction for that withholding tax.

On each scheduled payment date, PDAX Securities receives the coupon in USD and passes those dollars to PDAX. PDAX then converts the payment into USD* and credits the customer's account. Interest passes through both the broker and PDAX before it reaches the customer. Although the bond pays in dollars, the conversion leaves the customer with the platform's FX* asset as the credited balance.

At maturity, PDAX Securities receives the principal and final interest payment in USD. PDAX converts those proceeds into USD* and credits them to the customer's account using the same procedure. The documented redemption process ends with that USD* balance. It does not automatically return the stablecoin the customer used to fund the investment, and the FX product's conversion restrictions apply to the proceeds.

## USD* has its own terms

The [PDAX FX* guide](https://pdax.ph/learn/pdax-fx-usd-guide/) says each FX* asset represents a stablecoin chosen by PDAX, called its Reference Asset. A holder is not directly holding the underlying foreign fiat currency. The [product disclosure](https://pdax.ph/fx-product-disclosure/) currently names $USDG as the Reference Asset for USD*, but permits PDAX to choose another USD-denominated stablecoin. A customer buying USD* takes that exposure instead of continuing to hold the original funding asset unchanged.

The disclosure also gives PDAX alternatives for safekeeping the Reference Asset. It allows an equivalent amount of the underlying fiat currency or assets denominated in that currency. Those alternatives include treasury bills, bonds, securities and similarly denominated stablecoins. Naming $USDG as the current Reference Asset does not mean that PDAX holds every customer's exposure solely in that token throughout the process.

PDAX does not guarantee a strict 1:1 exchange ratio between FX* or its Reference Asset and the underlying fiat currency or assets used for safekeeping. For conversions other than direct dollar funding and withdrawals, the disclosure specifies the prevailing USD-denominated stablecoin rate at the time of conversion. Customers are told to review that rate on the confirmation page. This conversion price is separate from the bond's coupon.

The exit restriction is explicit: “Please note that you cannot convert USD* to USD-denominated stablecoins.” The disclosed dollar withdrawal route sends the resulting USD to the user's chosen US dollar account, excluding applicable bank fees and charges. Customers who use stablecoins to enter the bond investment therefore cannot reverse that route through a conversion of USD* back into the same token.

## Selling early and the offer deadline

Customers may sell before maturity on the secondary market, subject to fees, market conditions and availability, according to PDAX. The sale price can be above or below the original purchase amount. Once a customer sells, they lose entitlement to coupon payments scheduled after the sale. The stated annual coupon does not guarantee recovery of the purchase amount if the investor chooses to sell early.

PDAX's announcement says there are no PDAX fees until November 24, 2026. The temporary waiver is separate from the bank charges described in the disclosure and the conditions for secondary sales in the guide. The public offer is also scheduled to end on November 24, 2026, though the Bureau of the Treasury can close it earlier or adjust it.
