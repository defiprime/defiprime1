At 15:00 UTC on October 16, 2026, Jack Henry [announced an expanded Google Cloud collaboration](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/jack-henry-expands-collaboration-with-google-cloud-to-deliver-tokenized-deposits-for-banks-and-credit-unions-302911361.html) intended to let banks and credit unions offer tokenized deposits through Google Cloud Universal Ledger. Jack Henry said it will be the first core banking technology provider to integrate with the service.

The announcement describes a planned integration, not an available product. Jack Henry says it will become the first provider to connect with the ledger and that banks can use the connection to explore new transactional uses. It gives no production launch date, names no participating bank, and sets no date for a first tokenized deposit.

## What the integration is meant to do

Google Cloud Universal Ledger is a fully managed distributed ledger service, according to the [Jack Henry release](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/jack-henry-expands-collaboration-with-google-cloud-to-deliver-tokenized-deposits-for-banks-and-credit-unions-302911361.html). The service lets financial institutions tokenize and transfer money and assets on a shared real-time record. It also provides programmable settlement around the clock.

Jack Henry plans to connect that ledger to the technology environment used by its bank and credit union clients. The company says the connection is designed to modernize deposit products, preserve the relationship between an institution and its account holders, and add payment and settlement functions. The institution would continue to hold the primary deposit relationship while using tokenized infrastructure for value transfer.

The release identifies payment execution, settlement, and programmable workflows as the main functions. It says clients would receive instant execution at all hours of the year. Automated workflows could run across a growing network of institutional participants, currencies, and asset classes. The same infrastructure is intended to support automated settlement processes.

The proposed arrangement also addresses how institutions fund settlement. Jack Henry says automated clearing and multilateral netting can reduce the need for tied-up liquidity or prefunded accounts. The stated goal is to preserve core reserves and primary deposit relationships while an institution adds digital asset functions. The release does not specify the netting model, eligible assets, settlement finality rules, or how claims on deposits would move between participating institutions.

That distinction matters for treasury operations. A payment rail that requires prefunding makes a bank hold liquidity away from its primary reserve position before customers transact. Jack Henry instead describes clearing and multilateral netting that preserves core reserves. The announcement does not quantify the expected liquidity reduction or define a settlement interval, so institutions cannot yet compare the claim with an existing payment route. They can verify only the proposed mechanism and the absence of a stated prefunding requirement.

The same limit applies to programmability. The release promises automated workflows across institutional participants, currencies, and asset classes. It does not publish a programming interface, transaction policy, or list of supported instructions. A bank can therefore evaluate the intended operating model, but it cannot yet test how an instruction is approved, changed, reversed, or reconciled with its core ledger. Those details will determine whether the connection supports routine deposit operations or a narrower set of institutional transfers.

Jack Henry presents integration as another part of the product. Its release says the company's architecture should simplify adoption and reduce operating complexity. Google Cloud supplies the distributed ledger infrastructure. Jack Henry supplies the path into the core technology environment used by banks and credit unions. The announcement does not identify which core products will support the connection first.

## From cloud modernization to tokenized deposits

The two companies established their strategic relationship in 2022. The original relationship focused on Jack Henry's cloud-native technology modernization strategy. In June 2026, they announced plans for AI-driven security capabilities that combine Jack Henry systems with Google Cloud agentic defense tools for banks and credit unions. The tokenized deposit work adds money movement and settlement to that relationship.

Jack Henry says it serves more than 7,200 clients. Its customer base gives the proposed connection a different route to market from a ledger sold directly to one large institution. Community and regional banks could approach tokenized deposits through their existing core technology provider, although the release does not say how many clients are evaluating the product.

The design also differs from an institution replacing its deposit system with a separate digital asset stack. Jack Henry says its architecture will sit with Google Cloud's ledger infrastructure and preserve primary deposit relationships. For a bank operator, that framing makes integration, liquidity management, and settlement workflow the immediate questions. It does not resolve legal treatment, interoperability, or how a customer redeems a tokenized claim.

## What banks and users can verify next

For banks and credit unions, the promised benefit is access to programmable payment and value-transfer functions without moving the core customer relationship to another provider. The release says the network can cover institutional participants, multiple currencies, and multiple asset classes. It does not name those participants, currencies, or asset classes. An operator assessing the service still needs the technical interface, access controls, operating hours for connected banking systems, and the terms governing net settlement.

For depositors, the practical change depends on what a participating institution exposes. Jack Henry describes real-time execution, automated workflows, and new digital payment experiences. It does not announce a consumer wallet, a deposit token standard, or a redemption process. Until one bank defines those pieces, the release supports a distribution channel and infrastructure plan rather than a uniform product for account holders.

The next verifiable milestone is a named financial institution connecting to the ledger with production terms. Jack Henry has not provided a date for that step. A live implementation would need to show which deposits can be tokenized, when transfers become final, how multilateral netting works, and whether the claimed reduction in prefunding holds under actual settlement rules.
