On October 12, 2026, SynFutures [told users on Blast](https://x.com/SynFuturesDefi/status/2109639128220356789) to withdraw their funds to Ethereum mainnet before October 26. The protocol said anyone with a valid SynFutures balance should act before that date following Blast's shutdown announcement.

The notice applies directly to balances held through SynFutures on Blast. Users with a $USDB balance will see a withdrawal prompt in the SynFutures portfolio interface. SynFutures directed them to open the portfolio page, choose the withdrawal control and confirm the pop-up for their Blast balance. The protocol asked users who encounter problems to contact its team by direct message.

## The normal withdrawal route has an end date

Blast [announced its wind-down](https://x.com/blast/status/2106032805280891073) on October 2. The L2 said its operating costs exceed the revenue the network produces and that it sees no credible route to economic sustainability. Blast asked users to move assets to Ethereum mainnet, including balances in the Blast progressive web app.

The October 26 date separates two withdrawal methods. Until then, Blast says users can withdraw through its normal interface. Assets will remain recoverable after the deadline, but holders will have to interact directly with Blast bridge contracts on Ethereum L1. Blast said it would publish instructions for that contract route before the interface deadline.

SynFutures has adopted the earlier date as its own user-facing cutoff. Its notice does not say that a balance disappears on October 26. It says users should withdraw before Blast shuts down and repeats the deadline for anyone still holding funds on SynFutures. The practical change after that date is the route available to the holder. The standard Blast interface will no longer be the withdrawal path described by Blast.

Blast also said it would reduce its withdrawal delay to 24 hours. That shorter delay is part of the shutdown process rather than an assurance that withdrawals are continuously available. The network first plans to withdraw its Lido assets, a process it expects to take approximately one week. Blast said withdrawals would be temporarily unavailable during that work even after the delay was reduced. Withdrawals are supposed to resume with the new 24-hour delay once the Lido process finishes.

This sequence gives SynFutures users two timing constraints. They need the withdrawal service to be available, and they need enough time for the reduced delay to run before the normal-interface deadline. SynFutures did not state a final initiation time that guarantees completion before October 26. A user waiting until the last day could therefore face timing or availability risk that the two official notices do not resolve.

The notices also separate protocol access from chain access. SynFutures controls the portfolio prompt described to its users. Blast controls the normal network withdrawal interface and is managing the Lido asset process that can pause withdrawals. Ethereum L1 is the settlement destination and becomes the location for direct bridge-contract interaction after the deadline. A successful prompt inside SynFutures is therefore only the start of the route described by the teams. Users still need the Blast withdrawal path to be available and the resulting transfer to complete.

Blast did not give a precise start or finish time for the temporary pause in its shutdown post. It provided an expected duration for the Lido work and said service would resume afterward. SynFutures did not say whether its prompt remains visible during that pause. That gap makes early initiation more practical than treating October 26 as the first day to act.

## What SynFutures users need to check

The SynFutures flow begins inside the protocol rather than at the Blast bridge. The [instructions for $USDB holders](https://x.com/SynFuturesDefi/status/2109639130489766018) say the portfolio page will display a prompt when the account has a balance eligible for withdrawal. The user then selects the withdrawal button and receives a pop-up for the Blast balance.

The wording matters for accounts with several positions or asset types. SynFutures refers to a valid balance in its first notice and specifically names $USDB in the step-by-step post. It does not list every supported asset, describe how open positions are treated or say whether a user must close positions before the prompt appears. Holders should verify what the portfolio page recognizes rather than assuming every Blast asset follows the $USDB flow.

Users should also distinguish the SynFutures prompt from the later bridge-contract procedure. SynFutures describes a portfolio withdrawal before the deadline. Blast describes direct interaction with Ethereum L1 bridge contracts after the deadline. Neither statement says that SynFutures will provide a separate interface for the post-deadline contract route.

The destination is Ethereum mainnet. SynFutures tells users to withdraw there, and Blast makes the same request for assets across its network and progressive web app. The official notices do not describe transfers to another L2 or an exchange as part of the shutdown process. They also do not promise reimbursement for an incorrect destination or an uncompleted transaction.

Operationally, a holder should confirm that the account shown in the SynFutures portfolio is the account holding the Blast balance, read the amount in the withdrawal prompt and retain the resulting transaction record. Those checks do not change the protocol flow, but they establish whether the withdrawal was initiated before the interface changes. Any unexpected prompt, destination or request for credentials should be treated cautiously. Blast separately warned users about impersonation accounts and identified @BLAST as its official X account.

## What remains unresolved

SynFutures has not published a contract address, transaction-level guide or fallback procedure for balances that remain in the protocol after October 26. Its [final reminder](https://x.com/SynFuturesDefi/status/2109639133748486640) directs users with problems to contact the team and again asks them to withdraw before the shutdown date.

Blast's promised instructions for direct contract withdrawals are therefore the next operational document to watch. Those instructions need to identify the Ethereum contracts, the transaction sequence and the way users prove or select balances after the normal interface closes. Until Blast publishes them, the official path with the clearest steps is the SynFutures portfolio withdrawal before October 26.
