At 21:07:32 UTC on October 14, 2026, the SEC accepted [VanEck's Form RW](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2091159/000209115926000005/vanecklidostakedethereumet.htm) for the VanEck Lido Staked Ethereum ETF under accession 0002091159-26-000005 and file number 333-290918. The sponsor asked to withdraw the registration after deciding not to proceed with the planned distribution. It also confirmed that the registration never became effective and that no securities were issued or sold.

The request closes this filing rather than changing a live fund. VanEck told the SEC that no preliminary prospectus had been distributed. There are therefore no shareholders in this proposed trust who need a redemption process, asset sale or transfer as a result of the withdrawal. The product's planned creation and redemption system never reached an effective offering.

VanEck Digital Assets filed the request under Rule 477 of the Securities Act of 1933. It asked for the withdrawal to take effect on October 14 or at the earliest practicable date after that. The filing gives one ground for the request: the sponsor does not intend to proceed with distributing the trust's shares. It does not attribute the decision to the SEC, market conditions, Lido, custody or tax treatment. Matthew A. Babinsky, a VanEck Digital Assets vice president, signed the request addressed to the SEC's Division of Corporation Finance in Washington.

## What VanEck had proposed

The [original Form S-1](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2091159/000162828025045249/vanecklidostakedethereumet.htm), filed on October 16, 2025, described a fund designed to reflect the price performance of $stETH after trust expenses. The trust would have held $stETH directly, giving shareholders indirect exposure to $ETH through a security held in a traditional brokerage account.

The proposed trust was a passive vehicle. It would not have used leverage, derivatives or similar arrangements, and it would not have traded to profit from price changes. VanEck planned to value shares daily against a dollar-denominated $stETH reference rate. MarketVector, a VanEck affiliate, would select the top five eligible $stETH trading platforms by rank from the CCData Centralized Exchange Benchmark review.

Shares were to be created and redeemed in baskets of 25,000. The S-1 described cash subscriptions and cash redemptions, with the sponsor purchasing or selling $stETH through selected third parties. It also contemplated in-kind redemptions that would deliver $stETH to an authorized participant. Those operating details remained proposals because the registration did not become effective.

For a cash creation, an authorized participant would have delivered enough cash for the trust to buy the $stETH represented by the basket. The sponsor would then have purchased the token from a selected third party that was not the authorized participant. Cash redemptions reversed that flow by selling $stETH and paying the proceeds to the redeeming participant. In-kind redemptions avoided that sale by transferring $stETH itself. The withdrawal means none of those routes became available through this product.

The trust also proposed a custody boundary around the asset. Its S-1 said a third-party custodian would hold all $stETH and carry insurance, but warned that the insurance might not cover every loss. It separately stated that trust-held $stETH would not be leased or loaned. The planned return therefore depended on the token's price and the staking rewards embedded in its rebasing balance, reduced by trust expenses, rather than on a separate lending program.

The filing left several commercial terms blank. It did not identify an exchange, ticker, sponsor fee, completed seed investment or named $stETH custodians. The withdrawal arrived without an amended registration that filled those gaps. The SEC company record lists only the original S-1 and the Form RW for this filer.

## The staking exposure that will not launch

The proposed structure would have passed Lido staking economics into the trust. The S-1 says $stETH represents deposited $ETH and automatically accrues staking rewards through a rebasing mechanism. It expected those rewards to accrue to the trust through its $stETH holdings. Shareholders would have received the economic result through the amount and value of assets represented by their shares, after expenses.

That design also carried protocol risk. The registration warned that validator misconduct could expose the underlying $ETH to slashing. A slashing loss could reduce staking rewards and decrease the amount of $stETH. The filing also stated that staking rewards accruing on trust-held $stETH would be treated as taxable income under the cited US tax guidance.

VanEck did not plan to register the trust as an investment company under the Investment Company Act of 1940. The S-1 also said the trust would not be a commodity pool. Investors would instead have owned shares in a vehicle whose only intended asset exposure was $stETH, subject to the trust's custody, pricing, creation and redemption arrangements.

The withdrawal removes that specific brokerage wrapper before it began operating. It does not change Lido, $stETH balances, Ethereum validators or the rights of people who hold $stETH directly. It also does not require an unwind of trust assets because VanEck said no securities had been issued or sold under the registration.

## What remains unanswered

The Form RW does not explain why VanEck abandoned the distribution. It does not say whether the sponsor plans a different liquid staking product, whether it could submit a new registration later, or whether any exchange application associated with the proposal remains pending. The request names no replacement structure and gives no future milestone.

The next formal step is SEC consent to the withdrawal on the requested effective date or at the earliest practicable date afterward. Until another filing appears, the public record ends with VanEck's statement that it does not intend to proceed with the shares registered under file number 333-290918.
