On November 20, 2026, AMINA Bank [opened custody and on/off-ramp services for Open USD](https://aminagroup.com/press/amina-bank-becomes-first-european-bank-to-offer-institutional-access-to-open-usd/) to professional and institutional clients. The Swiss bank says the services are available now, giving clients a regulated banking route to the Bridge-issued dollar stablecoin.

AMINA calls itself the first European bank to offer institutional access to $OUSD. Its public product register lists hot custody, with trading, staking and loan collateral left unmarked. The launch gives clients a custody and funding route, though support in the register stops short of the bank's full product suite.

## What the bank has opened

The [available-cryptocurrencies register](https://aminagroup.com/available-cryptocurrencies/) is the product record behind the announcement. Its $OUSD row marks hot custody as the only supported activity. The release separately confirms on/off-ramp services. The unmarked trading column therefore leaves an operational question: AMINA has announced a conversion route but has not explained in the release how it differs from the trading service listed in the register.

AMINA says its existing stablecoin offering includes $USDC, $USDG and $RLUSD. Open USD adds a consortium-backed asset to what the bank describes as a stablecoin-agnostic approach. The announced access covers professional and institutional clients. AMINA has not announced access for every retail customer or in every jurisdiction.

FINMA regulates AMINA in Switzerland. The release's legal notice says products and services may not be registered, approved, offered or available in every jurisdiction and may face legal restrictions. The bank's claim to be the first in Europe describes its launch; it leaves customer eligibility subject to those restrictions.

Chief financial officer Mike Foy puts custody, reporting and compliance at the centre of the service. In the announcement, he says: “For corporate treasurers adopting a stablecoin for the first time, the question is not which token to choose. It is how custody, reporting, and compliance are handled.”

## Hot custody and the bank's controls

AMINA's [general custody documentation](https://aminagroup.com/individuals/custody/) describes its hot wallet as an online wallet for quick access and daily transactions. Customers manage the wallet through AMINA Online Banking or the bank's mobile app. The page documents the bank's existing custody service and how clients use it. It supplies no separate set of Open USD operating terms.

The bank says it uses hardware security modules and multi-party computation for hot-wallet storage. Its data centre is audited, and its Cyber Defence Centre monitors the infrastructure continuously. Access to the wallet requires a multi-authentication system. AMINA publishes these descriptions of its controls; they are not an independent security assessment of the Open USD integration.

System changes need approval from three separate people under what AMINA calls its “six-eyes” principle. The custody documentation also describes disaster recovery arrangements. Backup keys sit in separate secure locations disconnected from the internet. These controls cover administration of the custodial service and recovery within it.

AMINA says it segregates digital assets under Swiss federal law and holds them separately from its balance sheet. It lists professional indemnity and cyber insurance for the hot-wallet service. The general custody page also refers to loans and derivatives, but asset-specific support comes from the product register. Those general references do not establish either service for $OUSD.

## The stablecoin behind the banking route

AMINA's announcement dates Open USD's launch to September 30, 2026. Bridge, a Stripe company, is the initial issuer. Governance is intended to transition to Open Standard, an independent entity being established as the permanent issuer. AMINA names BlackRock and BNY among the institutions holding the stablecoin's reserves.

The [Open Standard site](https://joinopenstandard.com/) describes a model that sends nearly all reserve revenues to companies that grow adoption. Participating companies lead governance and act in their collective interest. The revenue distribution covers businesses using the asset. Under the issuer's legal terms, token holders have no direct or indirect property interest in the reserve assets.

Open Standard advertises minting and redemption without fees, even at scale. AMINA's release lists Ethereum, Solana, Base and Tempo as networks where Open USD is available. The advertised issuer pricing supplies no bank-specific tariff, and the network list leaves unspecified which chains AMINA supports for client deposits and withdrawals.

Bridge's [Open USD reserve page](https://reserves.bridge.xyz/ousd), last updated November 19, 2026, reports 979,075,848 tokens in circulation against $981,075,963 in reserve assets. The page labels Open USD 100.20% collateralized. Bridge publishes these figures for the stablecoin as a whole. They show neither AMINA's custody balances nor demand for the bank's newly opened service.

The reserve page reports an allocation of 12.2% cash and 87.8% Treasury assets. Its Treasury description includes money-market funds comprising Treasury-bill ladders with less than three months' duration. Bridge says it discloses reserves monthly and that a Big Four accounting firm supplies attestations under American Institute of Certified Public Accountants standards.

## Redemption still has issuer conditions

Bridge's [stablecoin terms](https://www.bridge.xyz/legal/bridge-stablecoin-terms/bridge-building-inc) make the processing window for sale or redemption orders conditional. The issuer says it will process an order within two business days after determining that the order complies with the applicable user terms. Factors outside its reasonable control may prevent or delay processing. This commitment covers the issuer's processing of orders; AMINA has not published it as a withdrawal service level.

The same terms allow Bridge to blacklist addresses under applicable law or its internal compliance policies. A blacklisted wallet can be restricted from sending or receiving tokens, and Bridge can blacklist it without advance notice. Those issuer controls continue to apply when a regulated bank provides custody of the stablecoin.

Bridge explicitly excludes Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation insurance and Securities Investor Protection Corporation protections for its stablecoins. AMINA's statements about segregated custody and its own insurance concern how the bank holds client assets. They do not describe insurance of the token's dollar value.

AMINA has not published an Open USD-specific fee schedule, a list of supported deposit-and-withdrawal networks or a processing timetable in its announcement or asset register.
