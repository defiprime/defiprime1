At 13:01 UTC on October 15, Stripe Crypto [announced](https://x.com/StripeCrypto/status/2110717778244018535) a collaboration with Plasma to launch multi-currency stablecoin cards. The companies said the cards are intended for people and businesses that spend across currencies and markets.

Plasma is serving as a design partner rather than only adopting a finished Stripe product. The companies are developing the card experience together, while Plasma plans to move to Stripe's stablecoin card infrastructure. Plasma described the objective as letting a customer spend in the local currency wherever they are.

## What the card infrastructure will support

Stripe said it is expanding its stablecoin card infrastructure to support more fiat currencies and stablecoins. The announced fiat set is USD, BRL, EUR, MXN and COP. The stablecoin set is $USDC, $OUSD and stablecoins eligible under the GENIUS framework.

The infrastructure is available in more than 30 countries today. Stripe plans to expand that reach to more than 100 countries in 2027. The announcement did not divide those markets by currency or say whether every listed asset will be available in every country.

Plasma currently supports money moving into and out of accounts in several currencies. Its announcement named USD, EUR, GBP and BRL among them. The collaboration is meant to make balances already held in those accounts spendable through cards, instead of requiring Plasma to build and manage the underlying issuing infrastructure itself.

The clearest example in the announcement concerns a euro balance. A customer holding euros would be able to spend in euros without first converting the balance through dollars. A business could issue cards denominated in currencies that correspond to its spending needs. Stripe and Plasma said this structure is intended to reduce conversions and foreign-exchange costs.

The first version remains a multi-currency card product, not a promise that all balances will be routed automatically from launch. Stripe said the companies are working toward a broader experience in which a cardholder can spend across several balances and the appropriate currency is selected at the point of purchase. That automatic selection is a later capability in the announcement.

## What changes for customers and operators

For a Plasma customer, the planned change is at the spending edge of the account. Plasma already supports holding and moving money across currencies. The partnership would connect those balances to a card, making them usable at purchase without forcing every transaction through a dollar conversion. The effect depends on the balance and purchase currency, but Stripe's euro example gives the intended path: euros held, euros spent.

For businesses, Stripe is offering the card layer as infrastructure. Henri Stern, Stripe's head of crypto and stablecoins, said the aim is to let businesses offer cards across currencies and markets without building that infrastructure themselves. The announced support for fiat and stablecoins means an operator can design a card product around the currencies its customers hold and the markets where they spend.

That setup separates the customer account from the card rails. Plasma provides the account product and works on the experience as design partner. Stripe provides the fiat and crypto infrastructure for issuing. The companies said the partnership combines those pieces to develop the card experience for cross-border use.

Plasma CEO Paul Faecks said the company is moving to Stripe's stablecoin card infrastructure as part of the project. His statement tied the design to customers whose finances span countries and set the target as local-currency spending. Stripe's statement framed the same work from the issuer side: cards across currencies and markets that a business can offer without building the card stack.

The list of supported assets also sets boundaries around the first announced capabilities. On the fiat side, GBP appears among currencies Plasma accounts can already move, but it is not in Stripe's initial five-currency support list for the expanded card infrastructure. On the stablecoin side, eligibility covers $USDC, $OUSD and qualifying GENIUS-compliant stablecoins. The announcement does not name additional stablecoins.

Country reach has a similar boundary. The infrastructure is available in more than 30 countries now, while the move beyond 100 countries is a plan for 2027. The companies did not provide a country-by-country rollout schedule in the announcement. Operators will still need to check where issuing is available and which listed currencies are supported in each target market.

Stripe's framing distinguishes account balances from card issuance. Plasma customers already hold balances in the account product. The planned card makes those balances spendable, while Stripe supplies the infrastructure businesses would otherwise have to build and manage. This gives the operator a way to match a card's denomination to its customers' spending currency. For the customer, the intended benefit is fewer unnecessary conversions when the held balance and payment currency match.

The planned automatic-selection feature would extend that model. Instead of a card tied to one selected balance, a cardholder could spend across several balances and have the appropriate currency chosen at purchase. Stripe described that capability as work in progress. The announcement therefore supports direct spending from named balances as the concrete product direction, while automatic routing across them remains a later step.

## The remaining rollout questions

Stripe called this collaboration the first step toward a broader multi-currency experience. The announcement does not provide a launch date for the Plasma cards, a fee schedule, or a date for automatic balance selection. It also does not state which of the currently supported Plasma account currencies will be enabled on the first cards.

The next dated milestone is Stripe's plan to expand its stablecoin card infrastructure from more than 30 countries to more than 100 countries in 2027. Before then, the practical milestone is the first Plasma card release and its exact combination of countries, fiat currencies and stablecoins.
