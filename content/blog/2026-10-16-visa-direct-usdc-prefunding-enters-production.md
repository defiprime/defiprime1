Visa Direct moved stablecoin-enabled prefunding from pilot to commercial deployment on October 15, 2026. [Visa named Inyo Global](https://usa.visa.com/about-visa/newsroom/press-releases.releaseId.22851.html), a Visa Direct processor, as the first company enabling the production service for eligible clients and partners. The initial implementation lets businesses served by Inyo prefund cross-border payouts with $USDC in near real time.

The launch changes the funding side of a Visa Direct payout. An eligible business can send $USDC to prefund payment activity instead of relying only on traditional funding windows. Inyo has added the option to the same platform its customers already use for onboarding, fraud and compliance management, payment acceptance, and domestic or international payouts. Recipients do not need a new integration described in the announcement because the stablecoin function sits on the prefunding side of Inyo's connection to Visa Direct.

## What moved from pilot to production

Visa introduced stablecoin prefunding and payout pilots through Visa Direct in 2025. Those pilots tested cross-border funding, liquidity management, and global money movement for eligible clients. The October release brings the prefunding capability into day-to-day commercial operations through a named processor rather than announcing another test.

Inyo is a payments infrastructure provider for money transmitters, fintechs, banks, and payment businesses. Its platform provides one connection for user onboarding, fraud and compliance controls, payment acceptance, and payouts. Visa says those payouts reach more than 180 countries through Visa Direct. The new $USDC option is part of that existing connection.

Robson Dantas, Inyo Global's chief product and technology officer and co-founder, said the service gives customers a funding method that is available around the clock. He also said it runs through the same Inyo APIs they already use. That detail matters operationally: customers using Inyo do not need to build a separate API surface simply to access stablecoin prefunding. Eligibility and geography still determine availability.

The production service is for prefunding cross-border payouts. Visa's release does not say that payout recipients receive $USDC, nor does it describe recipients using wallets. The stablecoin is the asset used by the eligible business to supply funds for payment activity. Visa Direct remains the payout network in the announced implementation.

The distinction from Visa's 2025 pilots is narrow but concrete. The earlier programs included both prefunding and payout experiments. The production announcement names stablecoin prefunding as the capability Inyo has put into commercial use. It does not announce production access to every function tested in those pilots. Operators should therefore evaluate the service as a new funding rail for an existing payout workflow, not as a general promise of stablecoin delivery to recipients.

## The operating model

Traditional cross-border payment providers keep funds available in the places and currencies where they expect to make payouts. Funding windows can constrain when those balances are replenished. Inyo's integration gives eligible businesses another route: they can use $USDC to prefund Visa Direct payouts in near real time instead of relying solely on those windows.

The announcement does not claim instant delivery to the final recipient. Its near-real-time language applies to prefunding. The release also does not publish fees, supported originating markets, settlement chains, custody arrangements, or the conversion path between $USDC and the currency delivered to a recipient. Visa directs prospective users to a representative because availability varies by eligibility and geography.

For treasury teams, the concrete change is the timing of available funding. Inyo says its customers can fund payouts around the clock through their existing APIs. That can reduce dependence on a bank's operating window when an eligible business needs to replenish funds, although the release does not quantify any reduction in working capital or cost.

For compliance and payment operators, the stablecoin route does not remove the controls already attached to Inyo's platform. The same connection handles onboarding, fraud and compliance management, payment acceptance, and payouts. Visa limits the offer to eligible clients and partners, and both footnotes in the release make availability conditional.

The announced customer set is also specific. Inyo serves money transmitters, fintechs, banks, and payment businesses. Visa does not describe a direct product for retail users or a self-service route for companies outside Inyo. The first deployment reaches eligible Visa Direct clients and partners through a processor that has already integrated the capability. Other businesses need both commercial access and confirmation that their payment corridor qualifies.

## What users and operators should check

A business evaluating the service first needs to confirm that Inyo and Visa Direct support its geography and use case. The public release gives the network reach as more than 180 countries, but that is the reach of Inyo's payout platform powered by Visa Direct, not a promise that $USDC prefunding is available in every one of those markets. Visa explicitly says availability varies by eligibility and geography.

Treasury teams also need the missing implementation details before comparing this route with bank prefunding. The release does not identify the supported blockchain, required wallet or custodian, cutoff rules for converting prefunded value, or fees. It does not state whether a business can redeem unused prefunding back to $USDC through the same API. Those questions determine how much operational change sits behind the simple promise of around-the-clock funding.

Visa Direct head Vira Platonova described the launch as the point where earlier experiments became live implementations. The company plans to discuss stablecoins at Money20/20 USA in the week after the announcement. The next useful disclosure will be the precise eligibility, geography, chain, custody, and pricing terms that turn the first Inyo implementation into a service other payment businesses can evaluate.
