On November 25, 2026, Aave [opened its savings app to the public](https://aave.com/blog/aave-app-launch) on iOS and Android, ending the waitlist and the requirement for a Ghost Pass. The launch advertises up to 7.25% APY, subject to eligibility and variable rates, after an early-access period that Aave says reached users in more than 170 countries.

The app supplies deposits to Aave lending markets and allocates them across supported markets, presenting the user with one savings balance. Borrowers post assets worth more than their loans and pay the interest that generates depositor earnings. The product puts a mobile account and bank connectivity in front of that lending activity. Aave's launch notice explicitly says it is not a bank and that funds have no government insurance.

## Funding and the advertised rate

Eligible users in the United States (excluding New York), the United Kingdom and the European Economic Area can connect a bank account for deposits and withdrawals. Aave says it charges no fees for those bank transfers, while warning that the user's bank may impose its own charges. Access remains regional: removing the waitlist does not remove the eligibility restrictions attached to the app and its funding services.

Stablecoin funding supports $USDC, $USDT and $GHO across eight networks: Ethereum, Arbitrum, Base, $BNB Chain, HyperEVM, Monad, Optimism and TRON. Supported assets vary by network. The announcement describes these as deposit networks, so the list should not be read as a promise that each asset has every possible withdrawal route.

Earnings begin when a deposit lands and compound every second. Limited-time promotions, including referrals, can increase an eligible user's rate. Aave says the app displays the current rate, active boosts and qualification requirements before a deposit. Its launch announcement qualifies the advertised maximum with variable rates and regional eligibility, rather than offering the same return to every account.

The [product disclosures](https://aave.com/legal/app/disclosures) describe how the displayed rate fits into the vault. The Aave DAO sets parameters including target allocation, supported stablecoins, safeguard thresholds, the fee framework, the user-facing rate set point and permissioned roles. Approved parameters remain in effect until governance changes them. The vault has a governance-controlled configuration, with no fixed-term savings agreement from a bank.

Fees also sit inside that configuration. The disclosures say the difference between yield generated by vault assets and the amount credited to users accrues as a yield-spread fee to the Aave DAO treasury. Some on-chain actions, including converting one stablecoin into another during withdrawal, can incur gas and swap fees payable by the user. The no-fee statement about bank deposits and withdrawals is narrower than a promise that every transaction through the product costs nothing.

## Withdrawals depend on market liquidity

The launch announcement says users can withdraw without lockups, notice periods or withdrawal penalties. The disclosures attach a liquidity condition: repayment and withdrawal depend on sufficient assets being available in the relevant lending market. High utilisation or stressed markets can delay withdrawals or make them temporarily unavailable.

Overcollateralisation also leaves a liquidation risk. If borrowers fail to repay, or collateral loses value faster than positions can be liquidated, bad debt can reduce the assets available to lenders. The disclosures warn that severe conditions can cause partial or total loss.

Permissioned roles can also restrict deposits or withdrawals, limit cross-chain transfers, apply governance-approved withdrawal fees and adjust rates within approved bounds. Protective actions can take effect immediately. Aave users have recently encountered protocol-level restrictions, including [market freezes following a security breach](/aave-freezes-markets-after-binance-breach.html); the app's own disclosures set out the controls applicable to this product.

Bank settlement adds a separate dependency. Fiat transfers pass through regulated providers and settlement systems, with compliance reviews, payment failures or provider insolvency among the disclosed causes of delay. Aave also specifies that the regulatory permissions held by Push and other providers cover their own services. Those permissions do not extend to the app or Stable Vault.

## Account recovery and Balance Protection

Users register with an email address or phone number and a password, without separately managing a conventional wallet or recovery phrase. Aave's [account architecture explanation](https://aave.com/blog/how-aave-accounts-work) says the signer key is created on the user's device, encrypted using the password and authentication credential, then stored in encrypted form on Aave's backend.

The account architecture uses Alchemy's Modular Account v2 smart-contract wallet with custom ERC-6900 modules. Aave lists gas sponsorship, transaction batching and requiring an additional signer for sensitive actions among the capabilities of that design.

The account includes limited permissions for moving incoming stablecoins into the vault. Aave says this lets funds start earning without requiring the user to reopen the app after they arrive, particularly when a bank deposit takes time to settle. This automatic deposit permission is part of the account design, alongside the user's control of the signing key.

Withdrawal destinations must be approved, and adding an address requires a one-time passcode check. Users can set up two-factor authentication for sign-in and recovery. A lost password can be recovered through a previously signed-in device or, when the user has opted in and completed setup, biometric recovery provided by CoinCover. Losing all credentials and recovery options can permanently cut off access to the assets.

Balance Protection is a separate, discretionary program for eligible customers, with enrollment status visible in the app. Benefits depend on its terms, eligibility requirements, coverage tiers and overall limits. The disclosures state: "The Balance Protection Program is not insurance, is not a bank deposit guarantee, and does not guarantee reimbursement for any loss."

Aave says additional deposit, sending and receiving options, more stablecoin networks and Auto-Saver tools will arrive in the coming weeks. The launch announcement gives no firm delivery date for those additions.
